Jobs and apprenticeships

    Salary sorting uses the lowest pay figure in each advert. Hourly pay is turned into a yearly amount for a 37.5 hour week so jobs can be compared. Pay is for a full time week of 37.5 hours. Part time jobs are paid a share of it. Details are summarised from each employer's advert, so check the advert before you apply. Jobs can close early if they get lots of applications.

    How to apply: 6 steps
    1. Pick a department. Use the Department filter to see roles in the area that interests you, such as wards, imaging or admin.
    2. Check what the job needs. Open "What you need and what you will do" on each job to see if it suits you now or what to work towards.
    3. Talk to your supervisor or programme lead. Tell them what you are aiming for. They can support you and may be able to give a reference.
    4. Apply on NHS Jobs. Make a free account at NHS Jobs (opens in a new tab) and use the advert link on each job. Apprenticeships are applied for through the link shown.
    5. Apply early. Jobs can close before the date shown if lots of people apply.
    6. Ask for help. The Careers and Employability team at the Learning Campus can help with applications. Email Sughra Kabir (sughra.kabir@nhs.net) or Cathia Bouyer (cathia.bouyer@nhs.net).

    Age and pay

    NHS Agenda for Change pay from 1 April 2026, for a 37.5 hour week. Source: NHS Employers pay scales 2026/27.

    NHS bandStarts atTop of bandExample entry roles
    Band 2£25,272£25,272Healthcare support worker, catering assistant, clerical officer
    Band 3£25,760£27,476Senior care support worker, therapy support worker, ward or team administrator
    Band 4£28,392£31,157Nursing associate (trainee route), senior admin, assistant practitioner

    NHS bands are paid the same whatever your age. A 19 year old and a 40 year old in the same Band 2 or Band 3 job are on the same pay scale.

    For other jobs, such as care homes, the legal minimum hourly pay from April 2026 depends on age: aged 16 to 17 £8.00, 18 to 20 £10.85, 21 and over £12.71. Apprentices under 19, or in their first year, get at least £8.00. Many employers pay more.

    Some roles have a minimum age, for example for night shifts, driving or lone working. Check the advert.
